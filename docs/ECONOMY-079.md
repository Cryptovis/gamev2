# Economy and data notes · 0.79

## Currency and balance

The engine stores money in **billions of US dollars**, preserving existing saves. The new formatter displays dollars, K, M and B. A generic finished tank has a $28M catalog anchor; import offers also have a 35% minimum premium over the estimated local marginal component assembly cost. These are authored game prices, not current quotes for a named weapon. Factory capital investment, running costs, components and production time remain separate.

Gold ($130M/tonne) and silver ($1.1M/tonne) are authored catalog anchors, not live spot quotes. Scarcity, escrowed supply and war premiums affect market prices. Other legacy resources use strategic game lots unless a unit explicitly says tonnes or vehicles. Existing budgets/GDP are game profiles and are not audited contemporary public-finance data.

An open listing escrows stock immediately. It pays no money on posting. A purchase debits the buyer and credits the seller for the actual filled quantity. Cancellation or expiry returns only the unsold quantity. Weekly AI buyers prefer affordable offers within 15% of the reference price. Sellers with low treasury may ask a discounted price; absurd asking prices can remain unsold. Bank gold remains distinct from mine output and industrial stock. Reserve release is an inventory transfer, not money printing.

Personnel mobilization is $25,000 per person, with $60/day personnel upkeep (authored). Domestic events are authored policy packages: festival $200M/7 days, sports $350M/14 days, food relief $150M/7 days plus 20 food lots, then a 30-day cooldown. These are national-scale programs, not single local events.

Embassy setup costs $3B and trade-framework setup $5B in the existing strategy scale. A player-origin proposal pays the full setup cost only after acceptance; an incoming proposal charges each government half. The discount is on setup costs, not on all future commodity purchases. Existing treaty and resource-contract duration mechanisms remain separate from retained post-office letters.

## Demographic model

Daily welfare depends on food coverage, GDP per person, stability, inflation, unemployment and taxes. Births depend on the preserved demographic baseline, welfare and taxes; food shortages/stability affect deaths. Poor welfare creates emigration pressure. Available stable destinations receive the same people removed from origin countries; blocked emigration is returned to the origin. Natural births and deaths remain genuine changes to world totals. Recruit counts reduce the civilian pool and workforce while keeping total population unchanged. This is a transparent game model, not a demographic forecast or a literal universal claim about taxation and fertility.
